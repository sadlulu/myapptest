Gemini Embedding 2 is an exceptionally strong choice for this exact use case. Because it is a natively multimodal model, it maps text, images, audio, video, and documents into the same continuous vector space.If you are building an extraction agent or a high-accuracy retrieval pipeline, this is a massive advantage. You don't have to run separate vision models for images and text models for documents, nor do you have to engineer a way to align their disparate vector spaces. It also consistently scores at the top of the MTEB (Massive Text Embedding Benchmark) leaderboard.What you are describing is a classic Multimodal Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipeline. Here is how the data flows:1.Ingestion and Embedding:Process your raw multimodal data and pass it through the Gemini Embedding 2 API. By default, this outputs a dense 3072-dimensional vector, though the model supports truncation to smaller dimensions (like 768) to save storage space without significantly degrading retrieval quality.2.Vector Storage:Store these vectors in a vector database (like Milvus, Pinecone, or pgvector) alongside the raw content chunks and any relevant metadata.3.Semantic Retrieval:The pinpointing phase.When a user asks a question, embed their text query using the exact same Gemini Embedding 2 model. The vector database performs a nearest-neighbor search (usually via cosine similarity) to find the vectors that are closest to the query vector, retrieving the top-k most relevant multimodal chunks.4.Generative Synthesis:Pass the user's original query along with the retrieved chunks into the context window of a generative LLM to synthesize the final answer.To answer your last question: Yes, you absolutely need another LLM at the end of the pipeline.Embedding models are strictly for representation and retrieval. They do not generate text; they only output arrays of floating-point numbers. To actually "ask questions" and get human-readable, reasoned answers, you have to hand the relevant content you just pinpointed over to a generative model (like Gemini 1.5 Pro or the newer 3.5 Flash) so it can synthesize the information.The embedding model acts as the librarian finding the exact right pages in the library; the generative LLM is the researcher reading those pages and writing the report.
